## Overview

Anxiety is a normal response to threat that becomes a health concern when it is
persistent, out of proportion, or interferes with daily life. Physical symptoms
are real: a racing heart, tight chest, dizziness, nausea, muscle tension and
disturbed sleep are common.

## Panic attacks

A panic attack peaks within about ten minutes and includes intense fear with
palpitations, breathlessness, trembling, sweating and a feeling of unreality or
fear of dying. It is frightening but not dangerous. Slow breathing with a longer
out-breath, grounding by naming things you can see and hear, and staying with
someone all help it pass.

## What may help day to day

- Keep a regular sleep and meal routine; caffeine and alcohol worsen symptoms.
- Move your body most days, even a short walk.
- Write down worries and separate what is actionable from what is not.
- Practise slow breathing or progressive muscle relaxation when calm, so the skill is available during panic.
- Reduce checking and reassurance-seeking gradually, ideally with professional guidance.

## When to seek help

Arrange an appointment if anxiety lasts more than two weeks, interferes with work
or relationships, causes avoidance of normal activities, or comes with low mood.
Seek urgent help if you feel unable to keep yourself safe.

## Ruling out physical causes

A first episode of palpitations, breathlessness or chest tightness deserves a
medical review, because thyroid problems, anaemia, abnormal heart rhythms and
other conditions can mimic anxiety. Do not assume anxiety is the whole answer
without assessment.

## Limits of this guidance

This page is educational and does not provide therapy. Talking therapies and, when
appropriate, medication are effective and are best discussed with a clinician.
