## Overview

Breathlessness is a normal response to exercise. It becomes a medical concern when
it happens at rest, when it wakes you from sleep, when it is new or rapidly
worsening, or when speaking a full sentence takes effort.

## Warning signs

- Being unable to speak in full sentences, or needing to pause to breathe mid-sentence.
- Blue or grey lips, tongue or fingertips.
- Using neck and shoulder muscles to breathe, or sitting forward to breathe.
- Chest pain with breathlessness, or coughing up blood.
- Sudden breathlessness with a swollen calf, or after long immobility.
- Drowsiness, confusion or agitation in someone who is breathless.
- Breathing that is very fast, or a pause in breathing.

Any of these means call emergency services.

## Common causes

Asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease flare-ups, chest infection,
pneumonia, heart failure, anaemia, a blood clot in the lung, a collapsed lung,
severe allergic reaction, anxiety and panic attacks can all cause breathlessness.
Several of these are emergencies, which is why severity, speed of onset and other
symptoms matter more than the pattern of breathing alone.

## What may help while waiting for help

Sit upright rather than lying flat, loosen tight clothing, and open a window for
fresh air. If the person has a reliever inhaler prescribed for asthma, help them
use it exactly as their own plan describes. Stay calm and speak slowly; panic
increases the work of breathing. Do not give food or drink if breathing is very
laboured.

## When to seek care

New breathlessness at rest, breathlessness that is getting worse over hours, or
breathlessness with fever, chest pain, swelling in one leg, or a change in
alertness needs emergency assessment. Breathlessness that comes only with heavy
exertion, improves with rest, and has been stable for weeks is usually assessed in
a routine appointment, where oxygen levels, heart function and lung function can
be checked.

## Limits of this guidance

A normal breathing rate at one moment does not exclude serious illness. If the
person looks unwell or is getting worse, seek emergency help rather than waiting.
