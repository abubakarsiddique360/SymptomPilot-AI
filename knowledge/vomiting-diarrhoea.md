## Overview

Most vomiting and diarrhoea is caused by a short-lived viral infection and settles
within a few days. The main risk is dehydration, especially in infants, older
adults and anyone who cannot keep fluids down. Preventing dehydration matters
more than stopping the symptoms.

## Signs of dehydration

- Thirst, dry mouth, and dark or strong-smelling urine.
- Passing very little urine, or no urine for eight hours.
- Dizziness or fainting when standing up.
- Sunken eyes, or in infants a sunken soft spot and fewer wet nappies.
- Confusion or unusual drowsiness in older adults.

Seek urgent care if fluids cannot be kept down for more than a few hours, if you
have not peed all day or are passing very little urine, if there is blood in vomit
or stool, if pain is severe, or if the person becomes drowsy or confused.

## Rehydration

Take small, frequent sips of an oral rehydration solution, which balances water
and salts better than water alone. If it is not available, diluted squash or a
light broth can help. Start with a teaspoon every few minutes after vomiting and
increase as it is tolerated. Continue breastfeeding or formula for infants.
Avoid very sugary drinks and large volumes at once.

## What may help

Rest, wash hands carefully, and avoid preparing food for others while unwell.
Eat bland food as appetite returns. Anti-diarrhoeal medicines are not suitable for
everyone, particularly with fever or bloody stools, so ask a pharmacist first.

## When to seek care

Seek review for symptoms lasting more than seven days, for a baby under one year
with repeated vomiting, for anyone with a long-term condition or weakened immune
system, for recent travel to a malaria area with fever, and for pregnancy with
diarrhoea and fever. Blood in the stool with cramping pain and fever needs urgent
assessment to exclude serious bowel infection.

## Limits of this guidance

This page describes supportive care and warning signs only. It cannot determine
whether vomiting is caused by infection, obstruction or another condition.
