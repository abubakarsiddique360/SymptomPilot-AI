## Overview

Burning or stinging when you pee, going far more often than usual, a sudden urge
that is hard to hold, cloudy urine and lower abdominal discomfort are typical of
a bladder infection. Most are uncomfortable but not dangerous. Infection that
reaches the kidney, or occurs in pregnancy or in someone with a weakened immune
system, is more serious.

## Warning signs

Seek urgent or emergency care if any of these are present:

- Fever, shivering or pain in the side or back with urinary symptoms.
- Vomiting and inability to keep fluids down.
- Confusion or unusual drowsiness, especially in older adults.
- Blood in the urine with clots, or difficulty passing urine at all.
- Urinary symptoms in pregnancy, in infants, or in people receiving chemotherapy.

## What may help now

Drink water regularly and avoid holding urine for long periods. Urinate after
intercourse and wipe front to back. A heat pack on the lower abdomen can ease
discomfort. Do not use leftover antibiotics or someone else's course; inappropriate
use drives resistance and may mask a more serious infection.

## Testing

A urine sample can confirm infection and guide treatment. Collect a midstream
sample in a clean container, ideally before starting any antibiotic. For recurrent
infections, a clinician may arrange further tests to look for structural causes.

## Prevention

Staying hydrated, emptying the bladder fully, and treating constipation reduce
recurrence in some people. Cranberry products have modest evidence for prevention
in some groups but do not treat an active infection.

## Limits of this guidance

This page cannot confirm infection, and not all urinary symptoms are infective.
Sexually transmitted infections, stones and irritation can cause similar symptoms,
so persistent or unusual symptoms need testing.
