## Overview

Colds and flu are viral infections. A cold usually builds over two or three days,
peaks quickly, and settles within a week to ten days, though a cough can linger for
two to three weeks. Flu tends to start abruptly with fever, aching muscles,
headache and exhaustion.

## What may help

Rest, drink fluids, and sleep enough. Warm drinks and honey can soothe a sore
throat in adults and children over one year. Saline nasal rinses reduce
congestion. Steam may ease a blocked nose. Antibiotics do not treat viral illness
and are only useful when a bacterial infection is confirmed or strongly suspected
by a clinician.

## Warning signs

Seek medical advice if you develop breathlessness, chest pain, confusion, a fever
that returns after improving, one-sided facial pain with a high fever, a stiff
neck, or if symptoms worsen steadily after day seven. Babies, older adults,
pregnant people and those with long-term conditions should seek advice earlier.

## Preventing spread

Wash hands often, cover coughs and sneezes, ventilate rooms, and stay home while
feverish. Annual influenza vaccination reduces severe illness in people at higher
risk.

## Antibiotic stewardship

Most sore throats and coughs do not need antibiotics. Unnecessary use increases
resistance and side effects without shortening a viral illness. If a clinician
prescribes antibiotics, complete the course as directed and never share them.

## Limits of this guidance

This page describes typical viral illness. Symptoms that are severe, unusual,
prolonged or worsening need assessment, because pneumonia and other bacterial
infections can start in a similar way.
