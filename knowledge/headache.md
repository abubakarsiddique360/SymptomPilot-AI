## Overview

Most headaches are tension-type headaches or migraine and settle without harm.
A small number signal bleeding, infection, stroke, raised pressure inside the
skull, or a problem with the eyes, sinuses, teeth or neck. The pattern and speed
of onset help separate the two groups.

## Warning signs

Seek emergency assessment for any of these:

- A sudden, extremely severe headache that peaks within seconds or minutes.
- Headache with fever and a stiff neck, or with a rash that does not fade.
- Headache with weakness, numbness, speech difficulty, confusion, or a seizure.
- Headache after a head injury with vomiting, drowsiness, or blurred vision.
- New headache with loss of vision, or a throbbing eye with a red eye.
- A headache that is worse when lying flat, coughing, or straining, especially with vomiting.

Same-day review is sensible when a headache is new after age 50, changes steadily
over days, wakes you from sleep, follows starting a new medicine, or comes with
pain in the jaw when chewing.

## What may help now

Rest in a quiet, dimly lit room, drink water, and eat regularly if meals have been
skipped. A cold or warm compress on the neck or forehead helps some people.
Simple pain relief can ease an occasional tension headache; use it sparingly
because frequent use of painkillers can itself cause daily headache. Keep a short
diary of timing, triggers and sleep, which is useful for a clinician later.

## Migraine

Migraine often brings throbbing pain on one side, nausea, and sensitivity to light
or sound, sometimes with a visual aura. Lying still in a dark room helps. Frequent
attacks, attacks that no longer respond to usual treatment, or a first-ever aura
after age 50 should be discussed with a clinician.

## Limits of this guidance

This page cannot distinguish a harmless headache from a serious one. Imaging and
examination are sometimes needed, and a normal scan does not remove the need to
report new neurological symptoms.
