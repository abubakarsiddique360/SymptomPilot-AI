## Overview

Abdominal pain — a stomach ache or belly pain — is one of the most common reasons
to seek care. Where the pain sits, how it moves, and what comes with it matter more
than how sharp it feels. Pain that starts around the navel and settles in the right
lower abdomen, for example, raises suspicion of appendicitis.

## Warning signs

Call emergency services or go to an emergency department for:

- Severe pain that stops you moving, or an abdomen that feels hard and rigid.
- Pain with fainting, very fast heartbeat, or cold, clammy skin.
- Vomiting blood, or black tarry stools.
- Pain in the right lower abdomen with fever and loss of appetite.
- Severe pain in the upper abdomen spreading to the back, especially with vomiting.
- Yellow skin or eyes with pain, or pain with a swollen abdomen.
- Abdominal pain in pregnancy, or after a blow to the abdomen.

Same-day assessment is reasonable when pain is worsening, localises to one spot,
comes with fever or repeated vomiting, or lasts more than a day without improving.

## Common causes

Indigestion, constipation, gastroenteritis, urinary infection, period pain,
ovarian problems, gallstones, kidney stones, inflamed pancreas, and bowel
inflammation all cause abdominal pain. Anxiety can also cause real discomfort.
Because the overlap is large, a clinician often needs an examination and
sometimes blood tests or imaging.

## What may help now

Rest, sip water, and avoid heavy or greasy meals until you know more. A warm
compress can ease cramping. Avoid painkillers that irritate the stomach if you
have a history of ulcers, and avoid giving any medicine for pain before an
assessment if you are heading to hospital, since it can blur the examination.

## For children and older adults

Young children may show pain by crying, drawing up their legs, or refusing food.
Older adults and people taking steroids or with weakened immunity can have
serious abdominal problems with little pain or fever, so a lower level of
symptoms still deserves attention.

## Limits of this guidance

Abdominal pain cannot be assessed from text alone. If the pain is severe,
worsening, or comes with any warning sign, seek emergency care instead of
monitoring at home.
