## Why urgency comes before diagnosis

Triage is the process of deciding how quickly someone needs to be seen, not what
condition they have. A single symptom can have harmless and dangerous causes, so
the first question is always how the person looks right now: are they awake,
breathing normally, and able to speak in full sentences?

## Signs that mean call emergency services now

Call your local emergency number immediately if any of the following is present:

- Unresponsiveness, or a person who cannot be woken.
- No normal breathing, or only occasional gasping.
- Chest pain or pressure that lasts more than a few minutes, or comes with sweating, nausea or breathlessness.
- New weakness or numbness on one side of the face or body, drooping face, or slurred speech.
- Severe difficulty breathing, blue lips, or being unable to speak in full sentences.
- Bleeding that will not stop after firm pressure, or vomiting blood.
- A seizure that lasts more than five minutes, or repeated seizures.
- A sudden, extremely severe headache, or a headache with fever and neck stiffness.
- Swelling of the tongue or throat, or a severe allergic reaction with breathing trouble.
- A fever in a baby younger than three months.
- Thoughts of suicide or serious self-harm.

While waiting for help, stay with the person, keep them warm, and follow the
instructions of the emergency dispatcher. Do not give food or drink to someone
who is drowsy or confused.

## Signs that mean same-day assessment

Urgent care or a same-day appointment is usually appropriate when a symptom is
worsening quickly, is not explained by a known long-term condition, or comes with
warning features such as a fever above 39 °C (103 °F), a fever lasting more than
three days, vomiting that prevents drinking, very little urine, dizziness on
standing, or pain that stops normal activity.

## Signs that usually allow monitoring at home

Mild cold-like illness, a mild headache, mild back or muscle aches, and
occasional loose stools are often safe to watch for 24 to 48 hours if the person
is alert, drinking, and passing urine normally. Monitoring means checking again
later rather than ignoring the symptom.

## What to record before you speak to a clinician

Note when the symptom started, whether it is constant or intermittent, what makes
it better or worse, other symptoms such as fever or vomiting, current medicines,
allergies, pregnancy status, and long-term conditions. This history helps a
clinician judge urgency far faster than a list of possible diagnoses.

## Limits of this guidance

This page summarises widely published first-aid and triage education. It does not
replace assessment by a qualified clinician, and it cannot rule out serious
illness. When in doubt, choose the more cautious option.
