## Overview

A mental health crisis is a moment when someone cannot keep themselves safe or
cope with their distress. Thoughts of suicide are common and treatable, and asking
directly about them does not plant the idea. Immediate safety and human contact
come before any longer-term plan.

## If you are having thoughts of suicide

Wanting to die, thinking about suicide, feeling you have no reason to live, or
feeling that others would be better off without you are all reasons to reach out
now rather than waiting.

- Contact emergency services or a crisis line now, and tell them clearly that you are thinking of ending your life.
- Move away from anything you could use to harm yourself, or ask someone to remove it.
- Stay with another person, or call someone who can come to you, and avoid being alone.
- Remember that intense suicidal feelings often pass even when they feel permanent.

If you have taken an overdose or injured yourself, call emergency services
immediately; some effects are delayed and need treatment before symptoms appear.

## Supporting someone else

Ask gently and directly whether they are thinking about suicide. Listen without
arguing or reassuring too quickly, and avoid judgement or promises of secrecy.
Remove or secure means of harm where possible, stay with them, and help them
contact a crisis service or clinician. Do not leave someone alone after a recent
attempt.

## Panic attacks

A panic attack brings sudden intense fear with a racing heart, breathlessness,
chest tightness, trembling and a feeling of unreality. It usually peaks within ten
minutes. Slow breathing with a longer exhale, grounding by naming objects you can
see and hear, and staying with someone all help. A first panic attack, or one with
fainting, chest pain or a persistent fast heartbeat, should be assessed to exclude
physical causes.

## When to seek ongoing help

Arrange professional support if low mood, anxiety, sleep problems or intrusive
thoughts last more than two weeks, interfere with work or relationships, or come
with changes in appetite and energy. Early treatment reduces the risk of crisis.

## Limits of this guidance

This page cannot provide crisis counselling. If there is immediate risk to life,
contact emergency services in your country rather than waiting for a reply here.
