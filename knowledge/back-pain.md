## Overview

Most back pain is mechanical, meaning it comes from muscles, joints, discs or
ligaments rather than from a serious disease. It usually improves within a few
weeks, and staying gently active speeds recovery more than resting in bed.

## Warning signs

Seek emergency assessment if back pain comes with any of the following:

- Numbness around the groin or buttocks, or loss of bladder or bowel control.
- Weakness in both legs, or a foot that drags.
- Fever with back pain, especially with a recent infection or injection.
- Back pain after a fall, or with known weak bones.
- Severe pain that is worse at night or at rest and does not change with position.
- A history of cancer with new back pain.

Same-day review is reasonable if pain follows a fall, radiates below the knee with
numbness, or comes with unexplained weight loss.

## What may help now

Keep moving within comfort, walk short distances, and gradually return to normal
activity. Heat can relax tight muscles. Avoid prolonged bed rest, heavy lifting and
sudden twisting. Simple pain relief may help short term; discuss ongoing use with a
pharmacist or clinician.

## Sciatica

Irritation of a nerve root can cause pain shooting down the leg with tingling or
weakness. Most cases settle over weeks. Seek urgent review for progressive
weakness, saddle numbness, or bladder and bowel changes.

## Prevention

Regular movement, strength work for the back and core, sensible lifting technique,
and breaking up long periods of sitting reduce recurrence. Most episodes do not
need imaging, and scans often show changes that are unrelated to the pain.

## Limits of this guidance

This page cannot tell whether back pain has a serious cause. New nerve symptoms,
fever, or pain after trauma need examination rather than self-management.
